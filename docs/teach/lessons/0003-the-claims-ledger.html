Phantom Link teaching workspace — Lesson 003 — [[MISSION.md]]

The Claims Ledger: what all 39 tickets let us say

Read time: 25 minutes. One win: for any claim about Phantom Link, you can name the ticket that proves it, the inference someone might draw from it, and the line you must not cross.

Rule of the house (same as Lesson 002): a claim without a test is marketing. This ledger separates PROVEN (a host test or hardware log exists), DERIVED (a reasonable inference a reader or recruiter might make — useful, but labelled as inference, not evidence), and BOUNDARY (the thing the proof explicitly does not cover). If you say a DERIVED line as if it were PROVEN, you have crossed the line.

0. How to read this ledger

MarkMeaningHow to use it
PROVENA named test, serial log, capture, or build artifact exists.Say it in docs, interviews, and the datasheet. Cite the ticket.
IN PROGRESS / REOPENEDWork is done but the acceptance gate has not fully passed.Describe the mechanism, never the result. "Fix applied, re-test pending."
OPENNo acceptance evidence yet.Do not claim capability. Say it is planned, in that order.
DERIVEDAn inference from proven facts that is useful in a marketing sentence but is not itself a measurement.Use it only with the hedge "this means / this implies", and keep the ticket basis attached.

9. Sentences you must not say

"Untethered / all-day battery operation." Tickets 22 and 23 are open. Say "cable-connected PoC; power-rig validation planned".
"24-hour soak passed" / "zero leaks over a day." Ticket 21 is open. Only short soaks (95 s, 1 h) are proven.
"MAC-layer SFO / covert channel." Our SFO is application-layer telemetry; the CYW43 MAC/PHY is untouched (15, 16, 29; Lesson 001).
"Independently reviewed / audited." Ticket 37 is open.
"Military-grade" / "NIST-approved crypto." Speck128 is a lightweight cipher chosen for this MCU, validated against published vectors.
"128-bit authentication" / "AES-encrypted header." The tag is 8 bytes (`2^-64`); the header is CTR-masked with session-derived keys, not AES-encrypted or separately tagged on the wire.
"The full ECDH public key is MAC-bound." Only its first 8 bytes are the confirm nonce; the rest is implicitly authenticated by the DATA tag (ticket 37 findings).
"Secure boot, encrypted boot, debug lock enabled." Ticket 35 is research only; OTP is untouched.
"Covert, undetectable traffic." Ticket 29 reduces trivial fingerprints; tickets 17 and 22 (sniffer report and airtime cost) are open.

10. Test yourself

Ticket 20 showed `persist seq=1 valid=1` after 70 s. Why was that not enough to close it?
A write happening once is a mechanism proof, not a persistence proof. The acceptance criterion is reading the record back after a reboot. Closing it required a physical power cycle and seeing `valid=1` on boot — which can only be set by `status_load()` finding a valid CRC on flash.
Why did the first flash fix produce `persist seq=4466` after 70 s?
The save interval was reset to `now` instead of `now + 60000`, so every loop iteration after the first minute wrote a page. The symptom looked like a counter bug but it was a scheduling bug; the fix is one line plus the correct interval constant.
The ESP32 saw no plaintext. Does that prove forward secrecy?
No. It proves a passive no-PSK observer sees ciphertext. Forward secrecy is a different property: a later PSK compromise cannot decrypt old sessions, and that is proved by ticket 31's KATs plus the hardware handshake, not by the RF capture.
Ticket 34 says "encrypted header". Can we claim the `proto_hdr` envelope with its separate 8-byte tag is on the wire?
No. The host module exists and is green, but firmware kept the 18-byte header and 8-byte auth field. What shipped is ECDH-key CTR masking of the header fields, authenticated by the existing tag. Say "masked with session-derived keys", not "AEAD-encrypted header with a second tag".
A recruiter asks if the link is "covert". What is the honest answer?
It is low-observable at the application layer: constant-length padded DATA, bounded-jitter decoys and a camouflaged SSID. It is not covert at the MAC/PHY layer, and there is no measured detection report yet (17, 22). Say "LPI-oriented", never "covert".
Which single ticket would let us claim "reviewed design"?
37. It is open. Until a reviewer actually completes the pack and findings are published, the datasheet must not use that word.
