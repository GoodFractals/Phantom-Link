Proof, then brag

Two $5-class boards. One source file. Forward-secret encrypted messaging that a password-less RF sniffer could not read.

This is not a tutorial project with a README full of adjectives. It is a working radio link that we attacked on air, broke on purpose, caught in the act, and fixed — then proved the fix on the same hardware. Every word above has a test number below it. Read the proof column and then say the brag column out loud with a straight face.

Receipts, no adjectives: 73 captured DATA frames, zero plaintext · 20 of 20 restarts recovered · under 8 s watchdog recovery · fresh key every session and every frame · 18/18 host tests under -Werror · three real firmware bugs found and fixed this week.

Plain English, confident on purpose. Each item says what we tested, how we know, and the sentence you can say out loud. If the proof line is empty, the brag line does not exist.

The rule: we flaunt only what we can reproduce. Nothing here says “reviewed”, “untethered”, “covert” or “24-hour soak passed” — those gates are still open. Bold claims with a log behind them are fine. Bold claims without one are marketing.

The 30-second version

Two $5-class Pico 2 W boards run one firmware image, each with a working UI. They build a private Wi-Fi link, confirm a shared secret, then throw that secret away for session keys that change every session and every message. We captured the radio with a sniffer that had no password and found zero readable messages. We can tamper with a frame, replay an old one, or use the wrong password — each is rejected and counted. Pull either board's plug and the pair reconnects in under ten seconds, twenty times out of twenty. Hang the firmware on purpose and the watchdog brings it back in under eight. And the boards remember their audit counters through a power cut.

For the person reading this resume

Most portfolios show you a repository. This one shows you a system: two devices, no PC in the middle, running crypto that changes keys faster than you can capture them, logging its own failures, and healing from a deliberate hang. Every impressive sentence here has a number you can click and a log you can read. That is the difference between “I implemented AES” and “here is the RF capture, here is the rejected tamper frame, here is the board that came back after I pulled its plug.”

We also say out loud what we have not done. That is not weakness — it is the posture of someone who has actually shipped something and knows exactly where the edges are.

The claims, one by one

TEST 01 Can two boards really be peers, not a screen bolted to a radio?

Proven One main.c, two CMake targets; IS_AP selects only Wi-Fi role. OLED, joystick, buttons, codec, parser and render path compile unconditionally. T2–T12 passed on both boards.

Brag Both devices can type, send, receive and render. Either one can be the access point.

TEST 02 Can an observer read our messages off the air?

Proven Ticket 14: WPA2 decrypted-path capture plus an ESP32 no-PSK RF capture. 73 AP DATA frames, zero plaintext hits. Logs archived with the build hashes.

Brag We put the link on air and attacked it with a sniffer that had no credentials. It found nothing readable.

Say “No plaintext in a password-less RF capture — 73 frames, zero hits.”

TEST 03 Does a stolen config secret unlock old conversations?

Proven Ticket 31: ephemeral X25519 key pair generated per session, ECDH mixed with PSK and session ID, on-device self-test gates LINK UP. Ticket 37 review found scalar reuse and we fixed it; BYE → re-link verified with a fresh session and wrong=0.

Brag Steal the config key and yesterday's conversation is still garbage to you. Every session mints its own key on the device — and when a review caught a case where it didn't, we fixed it and proved the fix.

TEST 04 Is one message key the whole session?

Proven Ticket 33: per-frame keys from pc_kdf(master, LE32(seq), 0x04). Hardware probe: STA tx_key=1, AP rx_key=1, zero rollbacks and zero crypto errors; 95 s soak clean.

Brag We don't reuse a traffic key across messages. One frame, one key — break one and the next is a brand new problem.

TEST 05 Can somebody strip the frame headers and track the session?

Proven Ticket 34: session ID, sequence and ACK are masked with domain-separated ECDH keys after the handshake, selected by a clear-prefix flag. 95 s soak with wrong=0, crypto=0. Host proto_hdr tests cover tamper, wrong key and wrong seq.

Brag A PSK-only leak no longer reveals who is talking or which frame is which. Say “masked with session-derived keys” — not “AES-encrypted header”.

TEST 06 What happens if we flip a bit in a message?

Proven Ticket 14 T16 and 32: hardware tamper probe flips one auth byte; AP rejects it with CRYPTO FAIL: bad tag and increments crypto exactly once. A valid frame immediately after still decrypts.

Brag Touch one bit of a message in flight and the receiver drops it, counts it, and keeps talking. We did this on real boards over a real radio — not a unit test in a vacuum.

Say “Tamper rejected on hardware, one error counted, next frame fine.”

TEST 07 What if someone replays an old frame or uses the wrong password?

Proven Ticket 14 T15/T17: replay gives RX stale with zero accepted; wrong PSK gives AUTH FAILED: HELLO bad tag and no LINK UP. Ticket 13 host tests: 10k replays accepted zero times, every single-bit flip rejected.

Brag Old frames are dead frames. Wrong key never gets a link. Both behaviours are on camera in the serial logs.

TEST 08 Can a forged frame break the key schedule?

Proven Ticket 37 review found that RX advanced the ratchet before checking the tag — a forged high-seq frame could wedge all later frames. Fixed by snapshotting the ratchet and restoring it on any verification failure. Re-verified with valid and tampered probes.

Brag Most implementations stop when the bad frame is rejected. We asked what that rejected frame changed, found a denial-of-service hole in our own key schedule, and closed it. That is the line between code that passes tests and code you can trust.

TEST 09 Can we use a modern cipher, or are we stuck with one?

Proven Ticket 32: ChaCha20-Poly1305 behind PHANTOM_USE_AEAD, RFC 8439 KATs, verify-before-decrypt, constant-time compare. Flashed both boards: valid DATA decrypted, tampered DATA rejected, zero auth/wrong errors. Speck remains default.

Brag The cipher is a build flag, not a rewrite. We verified both suites on silicon.

TEST 10 Does the pair survive a board rebooting?

Proven Ticket 18: 10/10 AP restarts and 10/10 STA restarts re-linked in under 10 s with fresh sessions; bounded backoff and epoch checks in the host state machine.

Brag Pull the plug on either unit and the other one finds it again. Twenty restarts, twenty recoveries.

TEST 11 What if the firmware hangs in the field?

Proven Ticket 19: a compile-time forced hang resets in under 8 s, logs RESET reason: watchdog, and re-links. Normal 1 h operation never tripped it.

Brag We deliberately broke the firmware and watched it heal itself faster than a human could notice.

TEST 12 Can we audit a run after the PC is gone?

Proven Ticket 20: STATUS prints every counter; flash ring writes one 88-byte record per minute; a physical power cycle booted with persist seq=4 valid=1 — valid can only be set by reading a valid record back from flash.

Brag The boards keep their own logbook. Unplug them, wait, plug them back in, and the counters are still there — proved on hardware, not assumed from the SDK docs.

TEST 13 Did we just write tests, or did we find real bugs?

Proven Three defects found and fixed this week: a 168-byte stack over-read in the flash writer, a save interval that wrote every loop iteration (4466 writes in 70 s), and the unauthenticated ratchet advance above. Each was diagnosed from sources/SDK docs and re-verified on hardware.

Brag The tests were good enough to make us doubt a passing result. We chased a “fixed” bug into a stale UF2 and found two more underneath it.

TEST 14 Is the traffic at least harder to fingerprint?

Proven Ticket 29: DATA padded to a constant 64 bytes before encryption with the real length inside the ciphertext; bounded-jitter decoys; SSID renamed to TMOBILE-1219; BSSID lock in ticket 39.

Brag Frames all look the same size and idle chatter is shaped. Call it LPI-oriented — never “covert”.

TEST 15 Is the build itself trustworthy?

Proven Ticket 01/02: host suite 18/18, both targets build -Wall -Wextra -Werror, boot banner carries a hash derived from main.c, and a fresh checkout needs no SDK path to run host tests.

Brag Warnings-as-errors, 18 host tests, and a source hash in every boot banner. Nobody has to take our word for which binary produced a log — the log tells you.

TEST 16 Did we need special access to the radio to pull this off?

Proven CYW43439 firmware is a proprietary blob; we used only public Pico SDK / CYW43 STA/AP and lwIP UDP interfaces. No firmware patch, no driver hack, no raw 802.11 injection (ADR 0002, ticket 29). Everything below the UDP socket is a black box to us.

Brag Our whole security protocol sits above a closed radio we were never allowed to look inside. Every guarantee is in our own code, portable to any board that gives us a UDP socket — and we are precise about what that costs: application-layer traffic shaping, never MAC-layer covertness.

Say “Black-box radio, public APIs only, all security in our layer.”

Why this is hard on a $5-class part

  • About 8 KB of stack: mbedTLS Curve25519 overflowed it in an lwIP callback, so ECDH was deferred to the main loop.
  • No RTOS: CYW43 PIO-SPI work, Wi-Fi state, OLED rendering and crypto share one cooperative loop with an 8 s watchdog.
  • Flash writes run with interrupts off; flash_safe_execute() and a full RAM page were needed to avoid a silent hang.
  • The radio stack is a proprietary firmware blob exposing only normal STA/AP interfaces, so MAC-layer SFO is unavailable; the side channel had to move up a layer without ever touching vendor firmware.
  • Two boards, one source file, zero warnings, and a host test suite that runs without the SDK installed.
Novelty, stated honestly: we have not found another published two-board PoC on a $5-class microcontroller that combines all of the above. That is our assessment from the work and the sources we have, not an independent market survey. Say “we have not seen this combination demonstrated on hardware at this price point” — not “the first ever”.

What we do not say yet

  • “Untethered / all-day battery” — tickets 22 and 23 are open.
  • “24-hour soak passed” — ticket 21 is running now; verify before claiming.
  • “Independently reviewed” — ticket 37 PR pending; machine-assisted is not human review.
  • “Covert” or “MAC-layer SFO” — application layer only; tickets 17 and 29 evidence pending.
  • “Secure boot / hardware-locked keys” — ticket 35 research only, OTP untouched.
  • “Military-grade crypto” or “128-bit authentication” — lightweight Speck, 8-byte tag.
